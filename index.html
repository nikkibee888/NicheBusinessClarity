4 Niche clarity

Define your spiritual niche

A niche is who, plus the problem, plus the transformation, plus your particular flavour. Specific is magnetic. Everyone is invisible.

The modalities you love. The lived experiences you have actually integrated. The things people already come to you for without being asked.

  • Modalities: tarot, reiki, Human Design, astrology, somatics, coaching, Gene Keys
  • Lived experience: burnout recovery, awakening, grief, creative blocks, money wounds
  • Your flavour: warm and witchy, structured and strategic, soft and intuitive, fierce and direct

Identity and life stage. Both matter. One without the other stays vague.

  • Identity: empaths, creatives, entrepreneurs, mothers, queer folks, neurodivergent women
  • Stage: awakening, post-breakup, business launch, midlife transition, new motherhood

What keeps them up at night? What are they quietly searching for at two in the morning?

  • "I feel stuck, blocked, lost"
  • "I am disconnected from my purpose, my intuition, my body"
  • "I am burnt out but I cannot stop"

After working with you they will feel a certain way and have a specific result in a specific area of life.

  • Emotional shift: panic to peace, doubt to devotion
  • Tangible result: first paying client, a business structure, a daily practice they keep
  • Identity shift: seeker to leader, hiding to visible

9 Soulful marketing

Simple, soulful, consistent

Not everywhere. Consistently visible in one or two places. Three pieces hold the whole system: a magnet, a home, and an invitation.

The magnet

A free piece of real value that draws the right people in: a mini reading, a meditation, a downloadable ritual, a short masterclass.

The home

One simple place where people land and understand what you do: a landing page, a Skool community, a start-here link in your bio.

The invitation

A weekly or fortnightly invitation to work with you. Posts, stories and emails that name your offer and say exactly how to book. Invitations, not convincing.

10 Launch

A gentle four-week launch

Soft but structured. Start with a beta cohort. Focus on conversations rather than counting posts.

Lock in the essentials before you tell a soul.

  • Finalise your offer name, promise and structure
  • Lock in your launch pricing
  • Set up your booking and payment link
  • Write your boundaries and onboarding document

Seed the problem and the desire your offer speaks to. No selling yet.

  • Share three story posts about the problem you solve
  • Email your list with a personal story
  • Message five warm leads with a genuine check-in, no pitch

Now you invite. Clearly, warmly, with evidence.

  • Public announcement post and email
  • Three to five behind-the-scenes stories
  • Personal messages to warm leads, with consent
  • Share one client transformation or testimonial

Onboard, over-deliver a little, gather feedback.

  • Send welcome emails and onboarding documents
  • Book kickoff calls
  • Request testimonials at the midpoint and at the end
  • Journal what worked and what you would refine
